# D–T Pusher: Energy, Radiation, and Compression Ledger

[← Implosion physics](README.md) · [D–T reaction record](../reactions/dt.md) · [Target architecture](../target-architecture.md)

This page describes what a burned D–T pusher could supply to the same ¹⁴N/H
reference core. It is an energy and path-length ledger, not yet an ICF drive
calculation. It does not assume that all D–T fusion energy becomes X-rays or
that radiation energy maps uniquely to compression.

## D–T energy partition

$$
\mathrm D+\mathrm T\rightarrow{}^4\mathrm{He}+n+17.6\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

Per D–T pair, 3.5 MeV is carried by the charged alpha and 14.1 MeV by the
neutron. Complete equimolar D–T burn releases

$$
\epsilon_{DT}=3.40\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}.
$$

The alpha can heat dense D–T locally when its range is short enough; the
neutron cannot simply be counted as pusher heat because it is much more
penetrating. X-rays are a secondary radiation field generated by hot pusher
plasma and radiating material, with a spectrum and conversion efficiency that
still need a driver model.

## Same-case geometry and D–T inventory

The worked core has peak radius 0.779 m and density about 998 g/cm³. The table
places a D–T shell immediately outside it at the provisional *compressed* D–T
density of 250 g/cm³. Thickness is a peak-compression coordinate, not an
initial cryogenic thickness.

| Compressed D–T thickness | D–T mass | Complete-burn D–T yield |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 cm | 19.3 t | 6.56×10¹⁸ J |
| 3 cm | 59.4 t | 2.02×10¹⁹ J |
| 10 cm | 216 t | 7.34×10¹⁹ J |

These values are large because the reference core is nearly two thousand
tonnes at peak compression. The decisive output is pusher burn fraction and
coupling, not raw yield alone.

## Radiation-energy temperature: a conversion bound

If fraction $f_{\rm rad}$ of D–T yield becomes an isotropic equilibrium
radiation field in the 1.98 m³ reference-core volume, then

$$
T_{\rm rad}=\left(\frac{f_{\rm rad}E_{DT}}{aV_{\rm core}}\right)^{1/4}.
$$

This is a normalization, not a claim of a blackbody spectrum or uniform core
deposition.

| D–T shell | 0.1% radiation conversion | 1% radiation conversion | 10% radiation conversion |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 cm | 3.94 keV | 7.01 keV | 12.5 keV |
| 3 cm | 5.22 keV | 9.28 keV | 16.5 keV |
| 10 cm | 7.21 keV | 12.8 keV | 22.8 keV |

## X-ray path length in the current N14/H core

At the reference density, the fully ionized N14/H card has electron density
3.21×10³² m⁻³. Its Thomson scattering mean free path is 46.9 μm. At a
representative 30-keV thermal-photon energy, the first free-free estimate is
an absorption length of about 0.47 mm.

Thus a keV X-ray field interacts in a thin layer relative to the 0.779-m core
radius. A surface-driven pusher does not justify an instantaneous uniform-core
temperature: its radial model needs an absorption profile and an inward
shock/ablation history. A central D–T ignitor needs the analogous calculation
outward from its hot region.

Electron scattering redirects photons but does not alone specify thermalization;
free-free absorption depends on spectrum, electron temperature, ionization,
and Gaunt factors. Energy-dependent opacity for compressed N/H is the next
required transport input.

## Relating pusher yield to compression

There is no valid scalar equation “D–T energy equals compression ratio.”
Compression needs pressure history, shell momentum, convergence, shock timing,
and losses. An energy ledger still gives necessary scale checks.

The reference core contains 7.95×10³¹ N14/H reacting pairs. Uniformly heating
all of it to 20 keV in the current ideal fully ionized closure would take about
3.82×10¹⁸ J. This equals 58% of the 1-cm shell yield, 19% of the 3-cm yield,
and 5.2% of the 10-cm yield.

That is not the proposed pusher requirement—the design should heat a much
smaller hotspot first. It demonstrates why coupling, not raw D–T yield, is
the key unknown. Define

$$
\eta_{DT\rightarrow h}=\frac{E_{\rm deposited\ in\ hotspot}}{f_{\rm burn,DT}M_{DT}\epsilon_{DT}}.
$$

The next pusher calculation should scan $\eta_{DT\rightarrow h}$, hotspot
radius, and deposition time. For every compression point it must report core
pressure/energy and pusher impulse or work, rather than total fusion yield
alone.

## Minimal coupled calculation to build next

1. Select compressed D–T thickness and density, then calculate mass.
2. Use evaluated D–T reactivity and shell dwell to calculate D–T burn fraction.
3. Partition yield into alpha, neutron, and radiative channels, with separate
   deposition models.
4. Apply a time- and radius-dependent deposition profile to the core.
5. Evolve core pressure/radius and test its reaction-specific burn front.

The current [central-hotspot front model](../analysis/burn-front-screen.md)
already supplies the reduced final step. This page defines the missing pusher
input ledger.
