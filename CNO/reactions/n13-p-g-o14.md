# 13N(p,γ)14O

[← Reaction records](README.md) · [Staged CNO pathway](../fuel-cycle/staged-cno.md)

**Role:** hot-CNO capture before cold 14O beta-decay storage.

## Required post-shot decay: $^{14}$O to $^{14}$N

$$
{}^{14}O\xrightarrow[T_{1/2}\approx70.62\ \mathrm{s}]{\beta^+\,/\,\epsilon}
{}^{14}N^*+e^++\nu_e,
\qquad {}^{14}N^*\rightarrow{}^{14}N+\gamma.
$$

The evaluated decay goes to $^{14}N$; most feeding is to its $2.31$-MeV
excited analogue state (historically evaluated at about $99.33\%$), which
promptly emits a gamma to stable $^{14}N$. Thus excited-state feeding changes
the gamma ledger but does not create a competing daughter isotope. The small
remaining decay feeding also ends at stable $^{14}N$. No later radioactive
decay limits storage; the next change is a deliberately loaded hot reaction.

This is a short processing delay, not a target dwell time: approximately
$3.9\ \mathrm{min}$ gives $90\%$ decay and $7.8\ \mathrm{min}$ gives $99\%$.

## Alternative hot pathways and leakage

The primary competitor is not an unrelated reaction but the intended breeder feed: $^{13}N$ survives the hot exposure and later beta-plus decays to $^{13}C$. Its fraction versus $^{13}N(p,\gamma){}^{14}O$ is the [primary mixed-path control problem](../fuel-cycle/n13-branch-control.md). Additional proton-induced channels at the high-energy end of the scan are unentered and must be checked before assigning the remainder to either useful branch.
