# 15N(p,α)12C

[← Reaction records](README.md) · [Staged CNO pathway](../fuel-cycle/staged-cno.md)

**Role:** charged-particle energy-release step that restores $^{12}C$ and emits one alpha/$^4He$.

## Reaction and products

$$
{}^{15}N+p\rightarrow{}^{12}C+\alpha,
\qquad Q\approx4.97\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

Unlike proton capture to $^{15}O$, this stage produces charged products. Their stopping relative to compressed-core radius determines whether this reaction directly heats the target or exports a substantial fraction of its energy. The alpha is also part of the shared $^4He/\alpha$ inventory shown in the [fuel-cycle graph](../fuel-cycle/README.md).

## Target and network context

This reaction occurs in the same hydrogen-rich solid composite and D-T-driven implosion described in [target architecture](../target-architecture.md). It follows cold $^{15}O$ decay from [the preceding capture](n14-p-g-o15.md) and returns $^{12}C$ to [$^{12}C(p,\gamma){}^{13}N$](c12-p-g-n13.md).

The pusher’s fast neutrons must be included in isotope leakage and energy-deposition accounting even though this reaction itself emits no neutron.

## Alternative hot pathways and leakage

$^{15}N(p,\alpha){}^{12}C$ is the desired carbon-regenerating channel. The competing $^{15}N(p,\gamma){}^{16}O$ route is especially important to quantify: it bypasses immediate $^{12}C$ regeneration but feeds an isotope already present in the breeder return leg, so it may be recoverable in-loop rather than permanently parasitic. Other proton-induced channels must be checked against the upper temperature scan before their products are classified.
