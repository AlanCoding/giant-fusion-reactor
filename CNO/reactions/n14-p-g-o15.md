# 14N(p,γ)15O

[← Reaction records](README.md) · [Staged CNO pathway](../fuel-cycle/staged-cno.md)

**Role:** hot capture stage in the common return loop. Its product, $^{15}O$, leaves the hot target for cold beta-decay storage before the next stage.

## Reaction and immediate products

$$
{}^{14}N+p\rightarrow{}^{15}O+\gamma,
\qquad Q\approx7.30\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

At the low incident energies relevant to thermonuclear burning, the capture energy emerges as one or more gamma rays rather than as a charged product. $^{15}O$ subsequently beta-plus decays with a half-life of 122.24 s:

$$
{}^{15}O\rightarrow{}^{15}N+e^++\nu_e.
$$

The neutrino energy is irrecoverably lost. Positron kinetic energy and annihilation photons are transport questions, not automatically local heat.

## Required post-shot decay: $^{15}$O to $^{15}$N

$$
{}^{15}O\xrightarrow[T_{1/2}\approx122.24\ \mathrm{s}]{\beta^+\,/\,\epsilon}
{}^{15}N+e^++\nu_e.
$$

The relevant evaluated decay is to stable $^{15}N$; there is no alternate
daughter isotope in the baseline fuel-cycle inventory. Electron capture and
positron emission are both included in the total decay mode, but they have the
same daughter. $^{15}N$ has no radioactive-decay clock, so it can be stored
until loaded into $^{15}$N$(p,\alpha)^{12}$C. The operational wait is about
$6.8\ \mathrm{min}$ for $90\%$ decay or $13.5\ \mathrm{min}$ for $99\%$.

## Core formulation and state

This reaction does **not** imply a fixed stoichiometric $N_2:H_2$ fuel mixture. $^{14}N$ is a catalyst that returns through $^{15}N(p,\alpha){}^{12}C$ and the carbon/nitrogen loop; ordinary hydrogen is the bulk feedstock. The core parameter to scan is $n_p/n_{14N}$, not a molecular recipe.

An ambient-pressure liquid $N_2/H_2$ mixture cannot be the baseline because nitrogen is solid at the temperature where hydrogen is liquid. Start instead with a hydrogen-rich solid composite or a layered solid core, as defined in [target architecture](../target-architecture.md). Until a specific microstructure and EOS are selected, do not assign it a single trustworthy density. The hydrogen phase constraint puts the initial target near 14–20 K; use the component densities and a porosity/mixing model to obtain $\rho_0$.

## Pusher and neutron exposure

The outer [D-T shell](dt.md) creates the implosion, not this capture gamma. The capture gamma can contribute to internal radiation only if the compressed core has sufficient energy-dependent optical depth; otherwise it escapes.

Each D-T burn also sends a 14.1-MeV neutron into or through the core. Those neutrons may drive unwanted $n+{}^{14}N$ channels or deposit damaging heat. They are not assumed negligible. The required calculation is a spectrum-dependent reaction/transport tally, compared with the number of $^{14}N(p,\gamma)$ captures completed in the core.

## Alternative hot pathways and leakage

The desired low-energy channel is $^{14}N(p,\gamma){}^{15}O$. At the upper end of the temperature/energy scan, other proton-induced channels and excited-state production can compete; their thresholds and rates must be loaded from evaluated data before assigning a burn fraction. Any branch that returns to a tracked CNO isotope can be classified as recoverable in-loop inventory; branches producing neutrons, light fragments, or nuclei outside the return map are parasitic until a recovery route is demonstrated.
