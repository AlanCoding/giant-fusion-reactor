# 12C(p,γ)13N

[← Reaction records](README.md) · [Staged CNO pathway](../fuel-cycle/staged-cno.md) · [D-breeder pathway](../fuel-cycle/deuterium-breeder.md)

**Role:** shared carbon entry capture and the physical branch point between staged-CNO energy production and catalytic deuterium production.

## Reaction and branch choice

$$
{}^{12}C+p\rightarrow{}^{13}N+\gamma,
\qquad Q\approx1.94\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

The hot target has two explicit initial-inventory cases:

- **One-proton case, $^{12}$C+$p$:** start with one proton per $^{12}$C. It
  supplies the first capture and is the reference point for a
  $^{13}$N/$^{13}$C-leaning output.
- **Two-proton case, $^{12}$C+$2p$:** start with two protons per $^{12}$C. It
  supplies both sequential captures in inventory accounting and is the
  reference point for an $^{14}$O/$^{14}$N-leaning output.

These are [the one-proton](../analysis/data/targets/c12-one-proton-primary.json)
and [two-proton](../analysis/data/targets/c12-two-proton-primary.json) target
cards, not fictitious three-body nuclear reactions. The elementary hot reactions remain this
$^{12}$C$(p,\gamma)^{13}$N capture and the dependent
[$^{13}$N$(p,\gamma)^{14}$O capture](n13-p-g-o14.md).

Neither case promises a pure output: the primary target can co-produce
$^{13}N$ and $^{14}O$. After disassembly and beta decay, chemical separation
routes $^{13}C$ to the breeder and $^{14}N$ to the energy/return inventory.
Proton inventory, temperature, density, and natural hydrodynamic burn duration
decide the product split. See [the dedicated branch-control analysis](../fuel-cycle/n13-branch-control.md). The capture gamma is not a replacement for the D-T pusher.

## Required post-shot decay: $^{13}$N to $^{13}$C

The breeder-bound fraction must be recovered after disassembly and held:

$$
{}^{13}N\xrightarrow[T_{1/2}=9.9584\ \mathrm{min}]{\beta^+\,/\,\epsilon}
{}^{13}C+e^++\nu_e.
$$

The evaluated total $\beta^+$ plus electron-capture branch is 100%, and it
feeds stable $^{13}C$. There is therefore no fractional branch into a different
daughter inventory in the baseline ledger. Electron capture versus positron
emission changes local radiation accounting, not the nuclear destination.
The daughter does not decay further; its relevant loss route is a later loaded
hot reaction, particularly $^{13}$C$(\alpha,n)^{16}$O or the competing
$^{13}$C$(\alpha,\gamma)^{17}$O.

The half-life is enormous compared with an implosion pulse, so decay is not a
hot-shot competitor. It is an operations wait: $90\%$ conversion requires
about $3.32$ half-lives ($\sim33\ \mathrm{min}$), and $99\%$ about $6.64$
half-lives ($\sim66\ \mathrm{min}$), before allowing recovery losses.

## Alternative hot pathways and leakage

The immediate alternatives are intentional, not parasitic: $^{13}N$ either receives a second proton while the expanding target remains hot, or survives to decay after recovery. Other $^{12}C+p$ channels are not yet entered; evaluate them only over the actual ion-energy range reached by the implosion and classify their products against the catalyst-return map.

## Target and network context

Use the common hydrogen-rich solid core and [D-T pusher](dt.md). This stage receives $^{12}C$ from [$^{15}N(p,\alpha){}^{12}C$](n15-p-a-c12.md) and is the root of both detailed pathways in the [reaction graph](../fuel-cycle/README.md). The proton-to-carbon ratio is a scan variable; carbon is catalytic rather than a one-pass stoichiometric reagent.
