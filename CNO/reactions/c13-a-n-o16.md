# 13C(α,n)16O

[← Reaction records](README.md) · [Primary 12C(p,γ)13N branch point](c12-p-g-n13.md) · [D-breeder pathway](../fuel-cycle/deuterium-breeder.md)

**Role:** later neutron-source reaction for the D-breeder. It consumes the $^{13}C$ recovered from the primary mixed-path shot and produces the neutron used by [`n(p,γ)D`](n-p-g-d.md).

## Origin of the 13C input

$^{13}C$ is not assumed to be made in a dedicated first-capture target. The primary $^{12}C+p$ implosion can produce a mixture of $^{13}N$ and $^{14}O$, with the split controlled by proton loading and integrated hot exposure. After explosive disassembly and cold storage, $^{13}N$ becomes $^{13}C$ while $^{14}O$ becomes $^{14}N$; chemical/isotopic processing then routes recovered $^{13}C$ here. See [13N branch control](../fuel-cycle/n13-branch-control.md).

## Reaction

$$
{}^{13}C+\alpha\rightarrow{}^{16}O+n,
\qquad Q\approx2.216\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

## Product timing and decay

This step does not require a beta-decay wait. $^{16}O$ is stable and is routed
to the later return captures. The emitted neutron is not an inventory that
should be held for decay: it must enter the hydrogen capture mantle promptly.
If it is not captured, a free neutron beta-decays with a half-life of about
$880\ \mathrm{s}$ to a proton, electron, and antineutrino—an unrecovered
breeder loss, not a useful alternate branch. The product neutron spectrum and
capture efficiency remain inputs to the blanket calculation.

## Alternative hot pathways and leakage

The desired branch is neutron production. $^{13}C(\alpha,\gamma){}^{17}O$ is the immediately relevant competing capture: it does not make D-breeder neutrons, but it produces an isotope already represented in the return network and may therefore be catalyst leakage rather than permanent loss. The relative branching at the proposed implosion conditions is unentered and must be evaluated.
